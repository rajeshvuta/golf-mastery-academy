Driver

Wood #1 | Distance: 230-300 yards

Loft Angle
8-12°
Club Length
45"

When to Use

  • Off the tee on long holes (par 4s and 5s)
  • When maximum distance is needed
  • On wide fairways with room for error
  • When there are no hazards in driving range

How to Hit

  • Ball Position: Inside your left heel (for right-handed golfers)
  • Tee Height: Half the ball should be above the club face
  • Stance: Feet shoulder-width apart, weight slightly on back foot
  • Swing: Wide arc with full shoulder turn, hit on upswing
  • Follow Through: Complete rotation with belt buckle facing target

Common Mistakes

  • Swinging too hard - tempo is more important than power
  • Teeing too low - makes it hard to launch high
  • Gripping too tight - causes tension and reduces swing speed
  • Not rotating hips - power comes from lower body

Pro Tips

  • Focus on hitting the sweet spot, not maximum power
  • Keep your head behind the ball at impact
  • Practice with alignment sticks to ensure proper aim
  • On tight holes, consider using a 3-wood for accuracy

3-Wood

Fairway Wood | Distance: 210-250 yards

Loft Angle
15-17°
Club Length
43"

When to Use

  • Off the tee on tight or shorter par 4s
  • Long approach shots on par 5s
  • From the fairway when you need distance
  • When accuracy is more important than maximum distance

How to Hit

  • Ball Position: Just inside left heel
  • From Tee: Tee low, just above grass level
  • From Fairway: Sweep the ball, slight downward strike
  • Stance: Slightly narrower than driver
  • Swing: Controlled tempo, 80% power

Common Mistakes

  • Trying to lift the ball - let the loft do the work
  • Positioning ball too far back in stance
  • Taking too steep of a divot
  • Swinging with driver tempo and speed

Pro Tips

  • Practice from good lies first before tight lies
  • Keep weight slightly forward at address
  • Focus on brushing the grass, not digging
  • Many pros use 3-wood more than driver for control
🌳

5-Wood

Fairway Wood | Distance: 190-220 yards

Loft Angle
18-21°
Club Length
42"

When to Use

  • Second shot on long par 5s
  • From the rough when you need distance
  • Approach shots from 190-220 yards
  • When 3-wood is too much club

How to Hit

  • Ball Position: 2-3 inches inside left heel
  • Setup: Ball on good lie or teed slightly
  • Swing: Smooth, sweeping motion
  • Impact: Ball-first contact, shallow divot
  • Finish: Balanced, full follow-through

Pro Tips

  • Higher loft makes it easier to hit from various lies
  • Great club for punch shots under trees
  • More forgiving than 3-wood for average golfers
  • Excellent for getting out of light rough

Hybrid (3H/4H)

Utility Club | Distance: 170-200 yards

Loft Angle
19-24°
Club Length
40"

When to Use

  • Replacement for hard-to-hit long irons (3i, 4i)
  • From the rough when you need height and distance
  • Tight lies where fairway woods are difficult
  • Approach shots from 170-200 yards

How to Hit

  • Ball Position: Middle of stance
  • Setup: Similar to iron setup, not wood
  • Swing: More like iron - slight descending blow
  • Contact: Ball-first, then turf
  • Versatile: Can hit like a wood or iron depending on need

Common Mistakes

  • Swinging like a wood instead of an iron
  • Ball position too far forward
  • Not taking advantage of club's versatility

Pro Tips

  • Most forgiving club in your bag
  • Perfect for high handicappers replacing long irons
  • Excellent for punch shots and escaping trouble
  • Works great from hardpan and tight lies
  • Many tour pros carry 2-3 hybrids now

Irons (3-9)

⚙️

Long Irons (3-5)

Distance Irons | 160-200 yards

3-Iron Loft
18-21°
5-Iron Loft
24-27°

When to Use

  • Approach shots from fairway on long par 4s
  • Second shots on par 5s
  • When you need a penetrating ball flight
  • Punch shots under wind or trees

How to Hit

  • Ball Position: 2-3 inches left of center
  • Stance: Shoulder-width, slight forward lean
  • Swing: Controlled, descending blow
  • Impact: Ball first, then divot after ball
  • Key: Compress the ball against the turf

Pro Tips

  • Many players replace these with hybrids - easier to hit
  • Practice with alignment rods for proper ball position
  • Focus on solid contact over distance
  • Lower loft requires more precise contact
🎯

Mid Irons (6-7)

Approach Irons | 140-170 yards

6-Iron Loft
28-31°
7-Iron Loft
32-35°

When to Use

  • Most common approach shots to the green
  • Learning proper iron technique
  • When you need accuracy and control
  • Versatile clubs for various situations

How to Hit

  • Ball Position: Center to 1 inch left of center
  • Stance: Feet slightly narrower than driver
  • Weight: 60% on front foot
  • Divot: Shallow, dollar-bill sized, after ball
  • Finish: Balanced, chest facing target

Pro Tips

  • 7-iron is the benchmark club - master this first
  • Practice distance control with partial swings
  • These are your scoring clubs - prioritize accuracy
  • Great clubs to learn proper ball compression
🔥

Short Irons (8-9)

Scoring Irons | 120-150 yards

8-Iron Loft
36-39°
9-Iron Loft
40-44°

When to Use

  • Short approach shots to greens
  • When you need high trajectory and soft landing
  • Tight pin positions requiring accuracy
  • Second shot on short par 4s

How to Hit

  • Ball Position: Center of stance
  • Stance: Narrower, more upright posture
  • Swing: Three-quarter swing often sufficient
  • Contact: Crisp, descending strike
  • Spin: Higher loft creates more backspin

Pro Tips

  • Perfect clubs for learning to attack pins
  • Practice specific yardages (125, 135, 145)
  • Work on trajectory control - high and low shots
  • Master these to become a great scorer
  • Focus on landing the ball on your target line

Wedges

⛳

Pitching Wedge (PW)

Approach Wedge | 110-130 yards

Loft Angle
44-48°
Bounce
4-8°

When to Use

  • Full swing approach shots 110-130 yards
  • Bump and run shots around the green
  • Partial wedge shots from 50-100 yards
  • Your go-to scoring club

How to Hit

  • Full Shot: Ball center, normal iron swing
  • Partial Shot: Shorten backswing, maintain tempo
  • Bump & Run: Ball back, hands forward, low finish
  • Weight: Slightly favor front foot
  • Contact: Ball-first contact is crucial

Distance Control

  • Full swing: 110-130 yards
  • Three-quarter: 90-100 yards
  • Half swing: 60-75 yards
  • Quarter swing: 30-50 yards

Pro Tips

  • Most versatile wedge in your bag
  • Practice three distinct yardages with this club
  • Great for learning short game fundamentals
  • Use for chip shots when green runs away from you
🎯

Gap Wedge (GW)

Attack Wedge | 90-110 yards

Loft Angle
50-52°
Bounce
8-10°

When to Use

  • Fills the yardage gap between PW and SW
  • Full swings from 90-110 yards
  • Controlled approach shots requiring accuracy
  • When you need more loft than PW but less than SW

How to Hit

  • Setup: Ball in center, square stance
  • Swing: Controlled, smooth tempo
  • Focus: Solid contact over power
  • Landing: One-hop-and-stop trajectory

Pro Tips

  • Essential for complete wedge system
  • Know your exact full swing distance
  • Practice half and three-quarter shots
  • Great for tight pin positions
🏖️

Sand Wedge (SW)

Bunker Specialist | 70-90 yards

Loft Angle
54-56°
Bounce
10-14°

When to Use

  • Greenside bunker shots
  • Full shots from 70-90 yards
  • Flop shots over obstacles
  • Soft pitch shots around the green

Bunker Shot Technique

  • Setup: Open stance, open clubface
  • Ball Position: Forward in stance
  • Entry Point: 2 inches behind ball
  • Swing: Accelerate through sand
  • Follow Through: Full finish, don't decelerate
  • Key: Use the bounce, don't dig

Pitch Shot Technique

  • Ball slightly back of center
  • Hands slightly ahead at address
  • Hinge wrists early in backswing
  • Accelerate through impact
  • High, soft landing with backspin

Pro Tips

  • The bounce is your friend - use it!
  • Practice from bunkers regularly
  • Higher bounce for soft sand, lower for firm sand
  • Master the standard bunker shot before trying flops
🌙

Lob Wedge (LW)

High Flop Specialist | 50-70 yards

Loft Angle
58-62°
Bounce
8-12°

When to Use

  • Flop shots over bunkers or obstacles
  • Tight lies requiring high, soft landing
  • Short-sided situations around green
  • When you need maximum height and spin

Flop Shot Technique

  • Setup: Wide stance, ball forward, open face
  • Hands: Level with or behind ball
  • Swing: Long, aggressive swing
  • Path: Swing along body line (out-to-in)
  • Face: Keep face open through impact
  • Finish: High, full follow-through

Common Mistakes

  • Decelerating through impact - leads to chunks
  • Closing the face - ball goes low and long
  • Not using enough loft - open it more
  • Trying flop shots when bump and run is better

Pro Tips

  • High risk, high reward club - practice extensively
  • Phil Mickelson's specialty shot
  • Only attempt when you have a good lie
  • Consider alternatives before attempting flop
  • 60° is most popular loft for this club
  • Not essential for beginners - add when ready

Putter

Scoring Club | On the Green

Loft Angle
2-4°
Length
33-35"

Putter Types

  • Blade: Traditional design, better feel, less forgiving
  • Mallet: Larger head, more forgiving, alignment aids
  • Center-Shafted: Shaft in center of head
  • Heel-Shafted: Traditional shaft position

Putting Fundamentals

  • Grip: Light pressure, hands neutral or weak
  • Stance: Feet shoulder-width, eyes over ball
  • Ball Position: Slightly forward of center
  • Stroke: Pendulum motion from shoulders
  • Face: Square at impact, no rotation
  • Tempo: Smooth back, smooth through

Distance Control

  • Length of stroke controls distance, not force
  • Practice lag putting from 20+ feet
  • Goal: Get within 3 feet on long putts
  • Focus on speed more than line on long putts

Reading Greens

  • View putt from behind ball and from side
  • Notice overall slope of green as you approach
  • Uphill putts break less, downhill putts break more
  • Grain affects break (ball breaks with grain)
  • Trust your first read

Practice Drills

  • Gate Drill: Two tees as gate for straight putts
  • Circle Drill: Make 10 putts in a circle from 3 feet
  • Ladder Drill: Putts at 10, 20, 30, 40 feet for distance control
  • One-Ball: Putt one ball, walk to next - simulates course

Pro Tips

  • 40% of your shots are putts - practice accordingly
  • Find a putter that suits your stroke and eye
  • Develop a consistent pre-putt routine
  • Focus on starting line - let speed follow
  • Confidence is key - trust your stroke
  • Never leave a putt short - "never up, never in"
